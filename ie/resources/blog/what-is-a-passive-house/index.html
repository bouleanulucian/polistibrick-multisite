What is a passive house and how does it work

The passive house is no longer an exotic concept — it is the European benchmark for efficient homes. Here is what it means in practice, without unnecessary jargon.

What does “passive house” mean?

A passive house is a home built to need very little energy for heating and cooling. It does not rely on expensive gadgets — it relies on an excellent thermal envelope, airtightness and controlled ventilation.

The Passivhaus standard (Germany / Europe) requires, in simplified terms:

  • heating consumption below 15 kWh/m²/year (3–4 times less than a typical new build);
  • airtightness verified by a Blower Door test (n50 ≤ 0.6 h⁻¹);
  • uniform thermal comfort, without major thermal bridges.

Why it matters to you

Energy bills have become as important as the price of the house itself. A well-built passive house can reduce heating costs by 70–85% compared with a traditional unoptimised brick home.

Over the long term, the initial investment difference pays back — especially when the structure is assembled quickly and predictably, as with the Polistibrick MBK + PBK system.

Common myths

“The house doesn’t breathe”

A passive house breathes through mechanical ventilation with heat recovery (MVHR), not through uncontrolled infiltration. Fresh air, minimal losses.

“It’s only for Germany”

The standard is climate-adaptable. In cold winters and hot summers, an MBK 270–300 mm envelope is often suitable — the architect sizes it precisely for your project.

“It costs double”

In practice, Polistibrick structure is +5–10% versus classic RE2020 (France), not +50%. Energy savings cover the difference in 8–15 years.
